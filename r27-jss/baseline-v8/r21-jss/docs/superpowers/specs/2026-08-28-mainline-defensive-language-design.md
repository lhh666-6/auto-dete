# R21 mainline and defensive-language revision design

Date: 2026-08-28

## Objective

Make the manuscript read as a confident methods-and-systems argument without weakening
its evidence boundaries. The narrative should foreground the certificate-bound admission
contract, the mechanism it requires, and the evidence that evaluates it. Repeated statements
about what the study does not prove should no longer interrupt that argument.

## Approved approach

Use a mainline-first, centralized-boundary structure:

1. Abstract, Introduction, Results, and Conclusion use the affirmative sequence
   **problem → mechanism → evidence → implication**.
2. Each experiment retains at most one local boundary sentence needed to interpret its
   estimand or denominator.
3. Detailed threats and exclusions are consolidated in Section 9, organized by evidence
   layer rather than repeated as lists of negated claims.
4. Method-defining negatives remain where they specify behavior, such as the absence of a
   model-callable fact-admission capability or rejection with authoritative-state stutter.
5. Related Work states the residual contribution positively after attribution; it avoids
   repeatedly announcing broad claims that the paper does not make.

The author additionally fixed six submission priorities: substantially compress the abstract;
preserve the four-failure necessity story; replace revision identifiers in the narrative with
baseline/AI-origin/harness/live-agent labels; move detailed hashes, receipt paths, and forensic
identities from the appendix to the repository; align the cover letter with JSS software
engineering for AI systems and architecture/verification/validation; and keep every claim centered
on authoritative-state admission integrity rather than general AI or prompt-injection security.

## Section-level edits

- **Abstract:** retain only the problem, contract, strongest formal evidence, live-agent result,
  performance result, and conclusion; omit the catalogue of every denominator.
- **Introduction:** preserve the four operational failures, the relation that distinguishes them,
  and the evidence chain that evaluates it. Do not revert to an empty-cell novelty argument.
- **Related Work:** retain precise attribution and one scoped synthesis paragraph; compress
  repeated novelty and guarantee disclaimers.
- **Problem/Contract:** preserve semantic contrasts and trust-boundary definitions, while
  moving general limitations to Section 9.
- **Evaluation/Results:** retain one concise interpretation boundary for each experiment;
  remove duplicate versions that restate Section 9.
- **Discussion/Threats:** consolidate limitations under construct, internal, external, and
  novelty-validity headings; state measured scope positively before exclusions.
- **Conclusion:** lead with the contribution and numerical evidence, then close with one
  compact applicability sentence.
- **Artifact appendix:** retain the exact input boundary and regeneration contract, while routing
  per-file hashes, receipt paths, and tamper-forensic detail to machine-readable repository files.
- **Cover letter:** frame the work for JSS as software engineering for AI systems, emphasizing
  architecture, verification, validation, and evidence-bounded authoritative-state admission.

## Non-negotiable evidence constraints

- Do not change any quantitative result, denominator, hash, citation, or experiment identity.
- Do not turn the generic materialization gap into a causal validation-cost estimate.
- Do not turn sequential trace comparisons into paired or hardware-independent speedups.
- Do not generalize one live-agent execution per scenario into a robustness or security rate.
- Preserve bounded-model, selected-projection, and finite-catalogue semantics.
- Preserve all author-controlled metadata and artifact-deposit placeholders.
