## P1 — Major Issues

### M1 [P1] Application evidence remains synthetic and operationally incomplete

- **Category:** Methodology / ESWA scope
- **Location:** `eswa/main_anonymous.tex:30`; `eswa/sections/06-results.tex:36-50`; `eswa/sections/09-limitations.tex:4-12`
- **Exact quote:** “The evidence supports the executable authority boundary and identifies concrete perception failures, but not production effectiveness: independently transcribed real forms and controlled reviewer studies remain necessary.”
- **Problem:** The scope boundary is honest, but ESWA application evidence still consists of two synthetic templates, no human participants, and only 60% complete-form success. Blur breaks QR identity and rotation breaks alignment systematically. This is the leading rejection risk, not a prose issue.
- **Fix:** Add access-controlled real-form validation and a reviewer study; meanwhile frame the article explicitly as architecture verification, not a validated industrial application.

**Risk judgment:** Synthetic-only evidence alone need not make the paper invalid, but combined with no human evaluation and a pipeline that completes only 60% of synthetic perturbations, it is likely to trigger rejection or a request for major new experiments.

### M2 [P1] Novelty is not established against adjacent governance architectures

- **Category:** Contribution / citation coverage
- **Location:** `eswa/sections/02-related-work.tex:19-20`
- **Exact quote:** “Prior work generally optimizes one of three layers: document inference, uncertainty-aware routing, or human interaction. Auto-Decte connects them through an authority model.”
- **Problem:** The comparison omits staged approval workflows, event sourcing, capability security, and access-controlled record systems. A reviewer can reasonably interpret candidate--fact separation as conventional approval plus provenance unless the manuscript states the closest alternatives and the additional guarantee precisely.
- **Fix:** Add a comparison table and citations contrasting guarantees, write capabilities, lineage, concurrency, abstention, and human authority with the closest governance architectures.

### M3 [P1] Direct-write verification is narrower than the invariant

- **Category:** Methodological rigor
- **Location:** `eswa/sections/05-experimental-protocol.tex:21-24`; `eswa/sections/06-results.tex:59-64`; `benchmarks/trust_faults.py:221-251`
- **Exact quote:** “all 12 attempted direct fact-write paths were absent from the three machine-facing services.”
- **Problem:** The code checks four guessed method names using `getattr`. Randomized values stress data handling but do not enumerate repository references, indirect calls, UI routes, migrations, or every capability capable of changing facts. The result is a useful interface check, not exhaustive noninterference evidence.
- **Fix:** Add a documented capability/call-graph inventory and tests over every fact-writing entry point; state that method-name probes are interface checks, not exhaustive noninterference evidence.

### M4 [P1] Joint QR-and-alignment rate is misstated

- **Category:** Internal consistency / numerical accuracy
- **Location:** `eswa/sections/06-results.tex:37`
- **Exact quote:** “The full pipeline identified the template and completed ArUco alignment on 80% of the 300 evaluation form--condition rows.”
- **Problem:** `form_raw.json` shows template identification on 240/300 rows and alignment on 240/300 rows, but both jointly on 180/300 rows (60%). The sentence naturally asserts an 80% joint rate.
- **Fix:** Replace with: “Template identification and alignment each succeeded on 80% of rows; both succeeded jointly on 60%.”
