Dear Editors of Knowledge and Information Systems,

Please consider our manuscript, “Review-to-Execution Continuity for Corrected Agent State Changes,” by Liang Hanghao, Xuan Wentao, Chen Qile and Peng Peng, for publication as an original research article.

The paper examines how authorization remains attributable when a state-changing agent continues after human correction. Equal final values and retained context do not establish that execution used the reviewed candidate instance. We distinguish the machine proposal, authorized correction, reviewed candidate and executed transition, and connect these roles through an executable contract covering instance binding, freshness, atomic successors and field-level provenance.

The evidence combines bounded formal checks, transactional implementations, controlled mechanism comparisons, historical correction-chain auditing and paired online continuations. The online evaluation reports the original frozen 512-arm collection and a separately frozen 117-pair supplementary deployment-A cohort with distinct denominators. Enforcing instance continuity turns standardized candidate substitutions into feedback followed by reviewed-candidate reuse or fresh authorization, with measurable continuation costs. Completion uncertainty remains explicit, and the constructed tasks and deterministic reviewer delimit generalization.

We believe this work will interest KAIS readers studying intelligent information systems, provenance and accountable agent execution. The contribution connects an executable authorization relation to observed continuation behavior. The accompanying public artifact provides frozen inputs, retained failures, offline scoring and analysis, source-to-result mappings, and verification receipts; an additional sensitivity analysis is explicitly identified as post hoc.
