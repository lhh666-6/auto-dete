Dear Editors of the Journal of Systems and Software,

Please consider “Authoritative-State Admission for AI-Derived Updates: Failure Distinguishability, Transactional Realization, and Evaluation” as a regular research article.

The paper addresses a software-engineering problem in systems where people correct AI-generated candidates before updates become authoritative records. It specifies the binding between the exact machine proposal, the human-authorized value and the complete successor's sources. Conditional history constructions, a transactional realization and executable controls clarify which distinctions the evaluated value-audit safeguards leave unspecified. The results are bounded to the declared observation model and evaluated systems; we do not claim universal superiority over database or governance mechanisms.

The manuscript connects the formal statement to implementation inspection, fault probes, persistence-equivalent cost measurements and restricted-agent integration. This is relevant to software architecture, verification and Software Engineering for AI Systems. The source, formal models, raw records and reanalysis materials are publicly available at https://github.com/lhh666-6/auto-dete/tree/r30-jss-2026-09-13. The repository distinguishes replayable checks from hosted-model generation, external harness dependencies and hardware-dependent timings.

Length justification: the manuscript contains 60 single-column preprint pages, including references, with a separate 20-page supplement. The main text retains the observation model and constructions, formal-to-concrete boundary, evaluated controls, methods and principal results so that the contract and its evidence can be assessed together. Detailed artifact procedures, stratified annotation analyses and the worked checklist example are in the supplement. We recognize that the manuscript is long and would consider further relocation of supporting material if requested by the editor.
