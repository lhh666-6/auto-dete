\title{Authoritative-State Admission for AI-Derived Updates: Failure Distinguishability, Transactional Realization, and Evaluation}

\author[inst1]{Liang Hanghao\corref{cor1}}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author. Email address: \texttt{zwu691403@gmail.com}}
\affiliation[inst1]{organization={College of Computer Science and Electronic Engineering, Hunan University},city={Changsha},country={China}}

\begin{abstract}
AI-generated candidates increasingly influence persistent operational records, yet a machine proposal, a human-authorized value, and a durable fact occupy different authority roles. We introduce \emph{authoritative-state admission} as the software relation connecting these roles. It binds an exact persisted AI candidate to an attributable human authorization and an explicit authorized value; under Correction, the candidate remains intact even when the admitted value differs ($x_c\neq x_a$). Under a declared failure and observation model, we characterize five observable information classes needed to distinguish equal-value candidate substitution, correction erasure, stale replay, partial successors, and source ambiguity. A valid nonempty batch creates one complete versioned successor atomically and retains a total per-field source relation. \system{} realizes the contract through separated capabilities, immutable authorization bindings, compare-and-swap-protected SQLite admission, and reverse-trace validation. Across two bounded Alloy scopes, all 66 commands matched their declared outcomes; eleven conjunct-removal operators exposed paired malformed witnesses, while an independent projection classified nine intended executions as SAT and twenty mapping mutants as UNSAT. A repeated live-agent benchmark covered three qualified model configurations, three prompt variants, fourteen scenarios, and 1,260 planned executions. Benign task completion was 320/335 behavior-evaluable runs (95.52\%; 95\% exact interval 92.72--97.47\%); 93 runtime failures are reported separately. The host-side contract rejected all 720 fixed invalid-tuple challenges without authoritative mutation, and 179 additional capability-unavailable checks also left authority unchanged. Together, these results support the five-class distinction basis, its transactional realization, and descriptive authority invariance under behavioral variation within the declared formal, implementation, and benchmark populations.
\end{abstract}
