Dear Editors of *Automated Software Engineering*,

Please consider our manuscript, "The Review–Execution Gap in Agentic Software: Specification, Design Space, and Empirical Characterization," for publication as a research paper.

Gerrit's `copyCondition` makes approval carryover an explicit engineering policy: it determines when a vote carries forward to a new patch set. Agent software faces the corresponding choice when a reviewed update is regenerated or crosses a queued handoff. Our manuscript specifies whether retained context, represented content, or the particular reviewed candidate permits approval reuse, and measures the resulting agent continuations under shared execution checks. Its technical foundation is a correction contract linking the machine proposal, authorized correction, reviewed candidate, and executed transition.

The manuscript makes four contributions for builders of agentic software:

- a design space and implementation questions for context, content, and instance binding, given a declared responsibility rule, shared checks, and immutable records;
- measured agent continuations after instance-binding rejection in frozen paired evaluations, including candidate reuse, fresh authorization, and added tool calls;
- an executable four-role correction contract specifying authorized values and field-source preservation in the installed successor;
- executable validation in relational and journal stores, with 165 controlled cases per mechanism, bounded Alloy checks, and a portable ToolGate implementation using normal callbacks plus an author-written commit bridge on tau2-bench Retail workflows.

The constructed binding-scope cases and the agent study answer separate questions. Both content and instance binding reject three semantic changes omitted by a narrower context key; instance binding additionally rejects three equivalent new instances. The agent study holds retained content fixed and measures continuation cost after identity-only rejection. With reviewed candidates available and a deterministic reviewer immediately approving valid reauthorization requests, all 32 delivered mismatch episodes recover: 24 use one commit, and eight request authorization and then commit. We distinguish these recovery actions from later confirmation calls and complete paired-suffix differences. Mutable external references require a separate consumption check. Native-flow probes characterize interrupt routing and explicit-edit boundaries in tested LangChain and LangGraph flows.

Recent ReSA and Guarded Commits preprints address approval scope, consumed versions, edits, and commit-time authorization. Our contribution connects binding choices to a specific correction relation and measured agent continuation under an isolated instance requirement. An obligation-level mapping distinguishes inherited mechanisms from this composition. Contrasting design examples explain artifact responsibility and content carryover without claiming deployment validation. Reported costs are agent-side; human re-inspection and approval waiting remain unmeasured.

The work fits the journal's interests in specification, design, execution, and validation. It studies agent approval gates, middleware, and persistence layers using constructed tasks and a deterministic reviewer.

The manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under consideration for publication elsewhere. Generative AI use is disclosed in the Statements and Declarations.
