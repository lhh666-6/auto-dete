\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What the contract adds to existing database mechanisms}

The admission relation specifies the information that must survive the transition from a proposal to an operational fact. Transactions provide an atomic boundary; compare-and-swap protects a predecessor; provenance records derivation; and authorization identifies permitted actions. None of these descriptions, taken alone, decides whether approval applies to a value, to a recorded context, or to one exact candidate instance. The contract makes that policy explicit and connects it to the construction and later interpretation of a complete record version. In the running example, the database must retain that the machine proposed 100, the human authorized 101, and an unchanged field retained its prior source.

The event-journal comparison is evidence of representational portability within the tested cases. Adding the exact review link produces the same 165 decisions and corresponding query answers as the reference implementation. This result prevents an architectural exclusivity claim: a journal can satisfy the contract, and a conventional schema can encode it through suitable keys, constraints, and transactions. The contribution is the stated relation, its conditional observation analysis, and the evidence linking enforcement to persisted outcomes. It is not a new transaction primitive or a demonstration that event sourcing is intrinsically insufficient.

Instance accountability is also a policy choice. The rich-context journal accepts 15 substitutions that preserve its declared value/context policy. Calling these errors without naming the stricter instance policy would prejudge the comparison. Conversely, the two exact-binding arms make 15 additional rejections under the looser policy. A deployment that treats equivalent extractions as interchangeable may accept that equivalence; one that must identify the exact reviewed artifact needs an explicit discriminator. Renewed approval without a changed value is a separate review-event operation because the concrete post-initial admission path creates transitions only for changed values.

\subsection{Where authorization must be established}

Candidate-bound storage preserves the authorization it receives. It cannot establish retrospectively which candidate a person actually saw. The original confirmation service constructs its authorization from the caller-selected certificate and value. The browser experiment shows why the trusted-caller assumption matters: request substitution can change that input before the database establishes a consistent binding. A server-held review session can bind an earlier review intention to the later request and rejects the nine tested request substitutions.

The three display-only substitutions accepted by both paths identify the remaining boundary. A server can receive the intended candidate while a manipulated page displays another one. The study therefore supports trusted presentation and authenticated-caller assumptions, rather than an end-to-end proof of human consent. The experimental session gate uses fixed principal labels and a separate session database; authentication, human attention, malicious-host resistance, and crash atomicity across the session and fact databases were not evaluated. The gate is an evaluated extension, not a property retroactively attributed to the original implementation.

\subsection{Interpretation of cost and provenance reconstruction}

The current measurements replace frozen v8 timings as the main cost evidence. They expose a substantial admission-cost difference between the reference implementation and the smaller exact-binding journal. The compared implementations differ in schema, ORM use, certificate validation, application functionality, and connection lifecycle. In particular, the journal connection is opened before the timer, whereas the reference ORM can establish its first connection within confirmation. The results describe these call boundaries; they neither isolate the cost of candidate binding nor establish a feature-matched system ranking. The prevalidated-materialization arm omits validation and planning and is not a safe replacement for full admission.

The trace comparison holds the current verifier fixed and changes three repository operations from bulk access to identifier enumeration and point loads. Equality of the entire returned object is checked after each timed call. Thus the measured difference concerns data access within this verifier. Twelve SQL statements across the grid do not imply constant total cost: transferred rows, in-memory indexes, and predecessor traversal still grow with the selected record's history and fields. Storage measurements characterize populated schemas; external evidence bytes, model-output files, and production retention policies are outside their footprint.

\subsection{Formal scope and implementation validity}

Proposition 1 is conditional on the declared history domain, observation projections, and outcome labels. In particular, an omitted observation cannot be recovered by decoding an opaque candidate handle. A different observation model can combine or derive these distinctions differently. The construction establishes neither universal minimality nor completeness over all admission failures. Alloy results apply to the encoded finite scopes, and several assertions check direct consequences of the definitions. The selected projection instances and mutants test the abstraction interface rather than prove a refinement theorem over every execution.

The reference implementation uses canonical JSON equality across change detection, authorized values, committed values, and tracing. The earlier integer/float and numeric/Boolean disagreement shows why change detection and provenance validation must share an explicit value-equivalence relation. The repaired regressions cover selected boundaries, not every serialization or migration behavior. The concrete prefix trace is stronger than the formal single-step anchor check in some respects; no-op admission and schema uniqueness also differ explicitly as described in \cref{sec:conformance}.

Trace completeness concerns persisted relations, not factual truth. Evidence comparison uses stored digests and canonical locators without re-reading external bytes. Out-of-band evidence replacement that leaves metadata unchanged requires immutable storage or an additional integrity check. Host authentication, serialization, policy state, hashing, and transaction execution remain trusted. Commit-entry policy revalidation rejects a revocation visible before that check but does not cancel a transaction revoked afterward. Other database engines, distributed revocation, deletion, redaction, tombstones, and retention expiry require additional design and evidence.

\subsection{Construct and external validity}

The 495 executions are a factorial set of constructed cases, not 495 independent applications. Twelve archived model/configuration--scenario cells contribute only four distinct proposal values; three synthetic type controls bring the total to seven. Their reuse checks deterministic admission semantics, not generalization across models. The event journal was separately implemented for this study, not supplied by an independent institution. Evidence contexts in these cases are constructed metadata, not a benchmark of image tampering.

The 60 browser cases use an instrumented local fixture rather than the full deployed interface or a user study. Fault counts are therefore coverage observations, not attack-success or user-error rates. The new studies make no hosted-model calls. Historical hosted-agent results are separate integration observations: task completion depends on the scoring criterion and missing runtime outcomes, while fixed host checks exercise restricted capabilities rather than autonomous model-generated attacks. Their original labels, failures, and author-involved annotations remain archived (Supplement S5).

Timing repetitions come from one Windows machine with uncontrolled background activity. Interleaving limits ordering effects but cannot remove scheduling, caching, or connection-lifecycle differences. Repetition-based intervals, where provided in the artifact, describe that run rather than uncertainty across machines or deployments. A separately implemented application with different records, reviewers, and operational policies would provide the most useful next test of transferability.

\subsection{Applying the relation}

A practical adoption starts by choosing the approval policy and tracing one correction through persisted records. Identify where the reviewed candidate is selected, where the authorized value is fixed, how the transaction rejects a superseded predecessor, and how changed and unchanged fields acquire sources. Retain both a legal control and a failure probe at each boundary. Supplement S3 provides a compact worked example and inspection checklist. Passing those probes is evidence for the exercised path; extending the contract to an independent system remains a separate validation task.
