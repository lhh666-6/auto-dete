Dear Editors of the Journal of Intelligent Information Systems,

We submit "Correction-Aware Data Admission: Candidate-Bound Authorization and Field-Level Provenance for AI-Derived Updates" for consideration as a research article.

Human-reviewed AI corrections introduce an authorization problem that cannot be inferred from the final authoritative value. The machine proposal, the human-authorized value, and the exact reviewed candidate are distinct objects. In particular, equal-valued candidates with equivalent retained review context can remain different authorization targets. Our manuscript identifies candidate-bound authorization as a correction-specific integrity requirement when instance accountability is required.

We specify an executable admission relation connecting the exact candidate and its authorization to an explicit authorized value, a fresh predecessor, a complete successor, and total field-level sources. Five safe/unsafe paired histories characterize the failure-distinguishing information in this relation. An independently written exact-binding event journal and a relational reference agree on all 165 corresponding cases, demonstrating realization across two persistence representations. Fifteen equal-valued substitutions accepted by context approval and rejected by both exact-binding mechanisms isolate the policy distinction between equivalent content and the reviewed instance.

The review-session experiment separates review-to-request consistency from presentation integrity; operational workloads characterize enforcement and provenance-query costs. The contribution is the correction semantics, information characterization, and executable policy separation, rather than a new transaction primitive or a performance claim. The Related Work discussion distinguishes this contribution from existing exact-proposal binding, approval, transaction, and provenance mechanisms.

The paper addresses the integration of AI-derived information, human review, and persistent data management within intelligent information systems. Online Resource 1 provides detailed formal, workload, validity, and reproduction records, with publicly deposited code and evidence. The manuscript uses the journal's smallcondensed LaTeX format and is 24 pages including references, tables, and figures.

The manuscript is not under consideration by another journal. Funding, competing interests, author contributions, historical annotation ethics, and AI-assisted preparation are disclosed in the manuscript.
