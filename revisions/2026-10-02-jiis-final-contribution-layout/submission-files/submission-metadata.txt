Abstract:
Human-reviewed AI-derived updates introduce a correction-specific authorization problem that is invisible in the final authoritative value. A machine proposal, a human-authorized value, and the exact reviewed candidate are distinct objects: even equal-valued candidates with equivalent retained review context can remain different authorization targets. We identify candidate-bound authorization as a distinct integrity requirement for corrected AI-derived updates when instance accountability is required. We specify a correction-aware admission relation connecting the exact candidate, its authorization, an explicit authorized value, a fresh authoritative predecessor, a complete successor, and total field-level sources. Safe/unsafe paired histories characterize five information classes whose omission can conceal different authority outcomes. An independently written exact-binding event journal and a relational reference realize this relation and agree on all 165 corresponding cases. A context journal accepts 15 equal-valued substitutions that both exact-binding mechanisms reject, separating content/context approval from instance authorization. A server-held review binding rejects nine request substitutions, while three display-only substitutions remain admissible in both paths, exposing the presentation boundary. The results establish an executable account of correction integrity across two persistence representations: preserve the proposal, commit the authorized value, and retain the exact reviewed instance and every field's source.
