\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Human-reviewed AI corrections introduce an authorization problem that cannot be resolved from the final authoritative value. The machine proposal, human-authorized value, and exact reviewed candidate retain distinct roles. Equal-valued candidates with equivalent retained review context can remain different authorization targets; when instance accountability is required, candidate identity participates in admission integrity.

We formalize candidate-bound authorization in a correction-aware relation linking the reviewed instance and explicit authorized value to a fresh predecessor, a complete successor, and total field sources. Five safe/unsafe paired histories characterize the information needed to distinguish the specified failure families. The independently written exact-binding event journal and relational reference agree on all 165 corresponding cases, while fifteen equal-valued substitutions distinguish them from context approval.

Server-held binding protects review-to-request consistency in the nine request-substitution cases; the three display-only controls identify presentation integrity as a separate boundary. Operational measurements characterize enforcement and provenance costs. The resulting contribution to intelligent information systems is an executable account of accountable correction: preserve the proposal, commit the authorized value, bind authority to the reviewed instance, and retain every field's source.
