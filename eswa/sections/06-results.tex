\subsection{RQ1: selective recognition}
Calibration selected $\tau=0.08$ for the target empirical selective risk of at most 1\%. On the disjoint 2,610-case evaluation set, the gate accepted 292 predictions and routed 2,318 to review. Coverage was 11.19\%, accepted accuracy was 100\%, and no erroneous auto-pass was observed. The Wilson 95\% interval for accepted accuracy was 98.70\%--100\%. Thus, the experiment observed zero errors among accepted cases; it does not establish a zero error probability.

Figure~\ref{fig:coverage-risk} shows the cost of this operating point. The full-coverage end of the curve has roughly 25\% selective risk, while zero observed selective risk first occurs at $\tau=0.07$ and increasingly reduces coverage. Risk control is therefore purchased with reviewer workload, not obtained for free.

The model comparison in Table~\ref{tab:baselines} is important for interpretation. The template recognizer achieved 74.90\% accuracy at effectively full coverage; forcing template prediction produced the same accuracy at exactly full coverage. HOG--linear-SVM achieved 99.81\% (2,605/2,610) at full coverage. Consequently, Auto-Decte's current recognizer is not competitive as a general classification contribution. The selective gate can isolate a small, high-confidence subset, while the stronger baseline indicates a direct path for improving coverage without changing the authority model.

Figure~\ref{fig:robustness} makes this asymmetric response visible: the conservative gate tends to abstain under severe degradation rather than preserve throughput.

\subsection{RQ2: trust-boundary fault injection}
All five injected faults left the pre-existing fact unchanged and yielded complete audit evidence (Table~\ref{tab:faults}). Wrong recognition and wrong AI suggestions remained candidates; irrelevant retrieval remained a reference. The stale review replay raised \texttt{ConcurrentReviewError}, and duplicate evidence raised \texttt{DuplicateEvidenceError}. These are five controlled application-path cases, not a penetration test or exhaustive security proof.

\subsection{RQ3: resilience and reproducibility}
The workflow completed with the AI adapter disabled, rejected duplicate evidence, rejected stale review, and returned a valid reverse trace. Across 30 measured repetitions, median cold service latency was 0.280 ms (IQR 0.071 ms; 95th percentile 0.517 ms), and median warm latency was 0.070 ms (IQR 0.014 ms; 95th percentile 0.129 ms). Figure~\ref{fig:latency} reports the distributions. These measurements validate low-overhead application services in the benchmark harness; they exclude perception and human interaction.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.72\textwidth]{figures/generated/latency.pdf}
  \caption{Application-service latency over 30 repetitions after five warm-ups. Values exclude image processing, UI rendering, and reviewer time.}
  \label{fig:latency}

The final verification run passed 70 tests, Ruff checks, and mypy checks over 65 source files. The artifact manifest reports 15 hashed outputs and a clean tracked source state at generation time. These checks support repeatability of the stated synthetic results; they cannot substitute for external replication or production validation.
