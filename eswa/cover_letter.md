Dear Editors of *Expert Systems with Applications*,

Please consider our manuscript, “Auto-Decte: Trust-Constrained Document Intelligence through Candidate–Fact Separation and Selective Prediction,” as a research article.

The manuscript addresses a practical gap between document-model accuracy and operational authority. Auto-Decte separates immutable evidence, append-only machine candidates, attributable human decisions, and versioned facts. Across the evaluated application paths, perception, retrieval, and optional language-model modules have no direct fact-writing operation. We combine this constraint with selective prediction and test both recognition behavior and the system boundary.

The evaluation combines a seed-disjoint cell benchmark, a whole-form benchmark, executable business workflows, and fault injection. HOG–linear-SVM achieved 99.81% accuracy on 2,610 evaluation conditions derived from 90 base units; uncertainty is reported with cluster bootstrap rather than treating all conditions as independent. Across 300 whole-form condition rows, complete success was 60%, with QR failure under blur and marker-alignment failure under rotation reported explicitly. All 60 evidence-to-export workflows produced versioned, reverse-traceable facts. Twelve attempted direct machine fact-write paths were unavailable, and all 500 randomized fault trials preserved existing facts. The paper’s contribution is therefore the trust architecture and its executable evidence, not a claim of recognition superiority. Production records were excluded because they may contain employee identity, payroll-adjacent values, and commercially sensitive data; the need for independently transcribed real-form and human-review studies is explicit throughout the manuscript.

The work fits *Expert Systems with Applications* because it presents and evaluates an expert-system architecture for consequential industrial document workflows, linking uncertainty-aware inference to auditable human authorization and provenance.
