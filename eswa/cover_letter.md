Dear Editors of *Expert Systems with Applications*,

Please consider my manuscript, "Auto-Decte: Trust-Constrained Document Intelligence through Candidate–Fact Separation and Certificate-Bound Human Authorization," for publication as a Full Length Article.

The manuscript addresses a practical gap between document-model accuracy and operational authority. Auto-Decte separates immutable evidence, append-only machine candidates, attributable human decisions, and versioned facts. Across the evaluated application paths, perception, retrieval, and optional language-model modules have no direct fact-writing operation. The system combines this constraint with selective prediction, and the evaluation tests both recognition behavior and the executable authority boundary.

The evaluation combines a seed-disjoint cell benchmark, a whole-form benchmark, an executable authority-integration benchmark, and fault injection. HOG–linear-SVM achieved 99.81% accuracy on 2,610 evaluation conditions derived from 90 base units; uncertainty is reported with cluster bootstrap rather than treating all conditions as independent. Across 300 whole-form condition rows, complete success was 60%, with QR failure under blur and marker-alignment failure under rotation reported explicitly. In the integrated system, recognition persists content-addressed candidate certificates; confirmations commit per-field decisions, transitions, and the record version in one compare-and-swap transaction; and reverse traces rebuilt from the database reported every injected corruption (tampered transition producer, deleted transition, and record-version rebinding) as incomplete. Cross-field certificate substitution and cross-form evidence were rejected before any write. All 60 clean evidence-to-export workflows produced versioned, reverse-traceable facts, and 500 randomized fault trials preserved existing facts. The paper's contribution is therefore the trust architecture and its executable evidence, not a claim of recognition superiority. No production-form corpus was available under an authorized research-use protocol, so the evaluation uses deterministic synthetic forms and a frozen reviewer artifact; the need for independently transcribed real-form and human-review studies is explicit throughout the manuscript.

The work fits *Expert Systems with Applications* because it presents and evaluates an expert-system architecture for consequential industrial document workflows, linking uncertainty-aware inference to auditable human authorization and provenance. The manuscript is original, has not been published previously, is not under consideration for publication elsewhere, and has been approved by the author.
