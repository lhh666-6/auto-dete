Dear Editors of *Expert Systems with Applications*,

Please consider our manuscript, “Auto-Decte: Trust-Constrained Document Intelligence through Candidate–Fact Separation and Selective Prediction,” as a research article.

The manuscript addresses a practical gap between document-model accuracy and operational authority. Auto-Decte separates immutable evidence, append-only machine candidates, attributable human decisions, and versioned facts. Across the evaluated application paths, perception, retrieval, and optional language-model modules have no direct fact-writing operation. We combine this constraint with selective prediction and test both recognition behavior and the system boundary.

The evaluation includes 4,350 deterministic synthetic image rows with disjoint calibration and evaluation seeds, seven perturbation families, three available recognition paths, five fault injections, and 70 automated tests. The manuscript explicitly reports that the 2,610 evaluation rows reuse 90 seed--font--digit base units across 29 conditions and are not statistically independent. We report the central negative result transparently: a HOG–linear-SVM baseline outperforms the built-in template recognizer at full coverage. The paper’s contribution is therefore the trust architecture and its executable evidence, not a claim of recognition superiority. Production records were excluded because they may contain employee identity, payroll-adjacent values, and commercially sensitive data; this limitation and the need for real-form and human-review studies are explicit throughout the manuscript.

The work fits *Expert Systems with Applications* because it presents and evaluates an expert-system architecture for consequential industrial document workflows, linking uncertainty-aware inference to auditable human authorization and provenance.
