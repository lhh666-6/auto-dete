\title{Auto-Decte: Trust-Constrained Document Intelligence through Candidate--Fact Separation and Selective Prediction}

\author{Anonymous author(s)}
\affiliation{organization={Anonymous institution},country={Anonymous country}}

\begin{abstract}
Document intelligence in payroll-adjacent and production workflows must control not only recognition error but also how machine output becomes an authoritative record. We present Auto-Decte, a trust-constrained human-in-the-loop architecture for structured industrial forms. Its central invariant separates immutable evidence, append-only machine candidates, attributable human decisions, and versioned facts: across the exposed application paths, perception, retrieval, and language-model modules have no direct write path to the fact plane. A selective recognizer abstains when a template-distance margin is below a calibration threshold, while a review action is the only transition that can create or revise a fact. We evaluate the implementation using 4,350 deterministic synthetic image rows, disjoint calibration and evaluation seeds, seven perturbation families, five injected trust-boundary faults, and 70 automated tests. The 2,610 evaluation rows comprise 29 conditions applied to 90 seed--font--digit base units and are therefore not statistically independent. A threshold selected on 1,740 calibration rows for at most 1\% empirical selective risk accepted 292 evaluation predictions (11.19\% coverage) with no observed accepted error. A HOG--linear-SVM baseline achieved 99.81\% accuracy at full coverage, showing that the contribution is governance of uncertain outputs rather than a new state-of-the-art recognizer. All five injected faults left facts unchanged and produced complete audit evidence. These results support the architectural mechanism while delimiting its evidence: field effectiveness still requires validation on independently transcribed production forms and human-review studies.
\end{abstract}
